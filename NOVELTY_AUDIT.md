# Novelty and positioning audit

Search date: 12 November 2026. This is a bounded novelty assessment, not a systematic review or proof of global priority. No assurance of acceptance by a particular journal is possible.

## Research contribution

The study quantifies the within-respondent increment from adding reported retinal photography to a dilation-specific annual eye-care question, then evaluates geographic and affordability differences under both definitions with paired design covariance. The 2025 data provide a current 20-state benchmark; a separate 12-state 2022 sample and seven-common-state analyses assess whether the classification pattern recurs. Incomplete pairs and different clinical meanings of contact and quality are explicitly retained.

## What is already established

- Retinal photography, telemedicine and autonomous AI can improve access in organized programs. Randomized studies and primary implementation papers are cited; this study does not introduce these approaches or measure their use.
- Earlier BRFSS and other surveys have extensively described low eye-examination uptake and inequalities. The geographic and cost gradients are not claimed as new discoveries.
- Wagner et al., BMJ Public Health 2025, examined both dilation and retinal-photography recency separately among adults reporting type 1 diabetes (doi:10.1136/bmjph-2024-002202). The present study is not the first BRFSS use of the photography item. Its focus is paired reclassification in all diagnosed diabetes and the change in measured disparities.
- The 2025 Ophthalmic Epidemiology paper (doi:10.1080/09286586.2024.2434247) examined 2017–2021 metropolitan differences in conventional dilated examinations, not the paired newer-item increment.
- Mississippi's official 2022 Diabetes report described separate dilation and photography proportions. It is an important descriptive predecessor: https://msdh.ms.gov/msdhsite/index.cfm/31%2C20735%2C110%2Cpdf/Diabetes_2022.pdf .
- CDC budget documentation already discussed photographic screening as a possible explanation for changes in dilation measures in the 2000s: https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/26714/cdc_26714_DS1.pdf . The general concern that dilation-specific measurement can omit imaging is therefore not new.

## Search record

Reproducible Europe PMC searches, exact URLs, retrieval date, hit counts and bibliographic metadata are saved in sources/novelty_search.json. The paired-modality query returned 20 records and the broader photography/BRFSS query returned 9. Many hits were meeting-abstract collections or unrelated analyses. Relevant indexed full-paper titles and the closest 2025 BRFSS article were examined; conference books were not exhaustively screened. Additional web searches combined BRFSS, photography, dilation, either, composite, surveillance, rural and the 2025/2026 date terms.

No exactly matching current paired-modality, design-based inequality analysis was identified in this focused search. Unindexed work, meeting abstracts, local reports, preprints and papers with different terminology remain possible. The manuscript deliberately makes no first-ever claim.

## Editorial assessment

The strongest framing is a short original investigation about diabetes eye-care measurement in the imaging era. The main result is a reproducible four-to-five-point increment and the persistence of access differences, rather than a new hormone mechanism or a drug effect. This is a clinically relevant endocrinology–ophthalmology intersection, but it does not directly measure gut hormones or incretin treatment.

Ophthalmology or diabetes services journals are plausible audiences. Top-field submission is an author decision; the limited state coverage, substantial item uncertainty and lack of clinical validation constrain competitiveness. A linked clinical-record validation study would strengthen the central measurement interpretation. A significant coefficient alone would not overcome these limitations.
