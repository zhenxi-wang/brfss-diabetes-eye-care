# Access and coding notes

12 November 2026, before focused outcome analysis. Direct requests to the main www.cdc.gov host returned HTTP 403. The same public files were accessible on the official portugues.cdc.gov host. Canonical and retrieval URLs and exact hashes are recorded separately. Both archives match the byte sizes and SHA-256 values published by an independent public preservation manifest, inspected as an additional provenance check; the analysis files themselves were downloaded from CDC.

The official codebooks are HTML inside ZIP archives, not PDFs. The photographic-recency variable is **DIABEYE1**, which must not be confused with the older DIABEYE question about retinopathy diagnosis. Annual values 1/2 and two-year values 1/2/3 were confirmed in both codebooks. _RACEGR3 in 2025 and _RACEGR4 in 2022 have compatible five-category codes; non-Hispanic other and multiracial categories are combined for precision, and unknown race remains missing in adjusted analyses. The imputed race variable is not substituted. _METSTAT is metropolitan/nonmetropolitan county status and is not a direct measure of remoteness, residence in a rural census tract, or provider supply.

CDC lists 20 US states using the full questionnaire in 2025 and 12 in 2022. Ohio requires version-specific weights in 2025; California, Maryland, Massachusetts, New York and Ohio require version-specific files in 2022. These are excluded as specified, without selecting on results. Territories are also excluded. Seven states occur in both analytic samples. Full original files are retained for denominator and coding verification. Some interviews in the 2025 survey file occurred in early 2026; the study is described by survey year rather than claiming every interview occurred in calendar 2025.

The CGM feasibility question was not pursued because the latest module no longer includes the earlier monitoring-frequency item. The selected research question and local protocol preceded focused BRFSS outcome extraction. No results or significance thresholds were used to select a different sample or hypothesis.
